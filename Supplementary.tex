\section{URL-Intrinsic Feature Representation}

LitePhish derives all inference variables from the observed URL string. It does not retrieve webpage content, resolve redirect chains, query domain-registration records, or use online reputation services. The representation combines 86 handcrafted lexical, structural, compositional, transition, heuristic, and entropy descriptors with character 2-grams and 3-grams. The handcrafted descriptors summarize interpretable URL morphology, whereas the N-gram representation captures short local sequences that aggregate descriptors may not express. These variables are statistical indicators rather than causal evidence of maliciousness; their interpretation depends on the training distribution and the surrounding feature context.

Table~\ref{tab:features} defines the 86 handcrafted descriptors. Ratio denominators are protected against division by zero. Entropy variables use Shannon entropy on the indicated character sequence, and Boolean indicators take values in $\{0,1\}$. Terms such as ``suspicious'' or ``risk'' denote fixed URL-intrinsic lexical rules; they do not imply an external reputation lookup.

\section{Training-Only Parameter Selection}

All representation fitting uses the source training partition, while hyperparameter selection uses only the source training and validation partitions. Validation phishing-class F1 selects \((\gamma,\alpha)=(0.55,0.90)\) when training LitePhish on PhishStorm, \((0.30,0.95)\) when training on Ebbu2017, and \((0.55,0.95)\) when training on PhishFusion. Target-dataset labels are not used for feature selection, model selection, or fitting. LitePhish retains 568 selected features when trained on PhishFusion; the source-specific cross-dataset configurations retain 939 features for PhishStorm and 821 for Ebbu2017.

Character 2-/3-gram selection uses \(d_{\min}=5\), \(r_{\max}=1.0\), variance threshold \(\tau=10^{-5}\), composite weight \(\beta=0.5\), L1-logistic inverse-regularization \(C_{\mathrm{ng}}=0.5\), and \(K_{\mathrm{ng}}=5,000\). SDCS uses \(S=20\) subsamples, a subsampling fraction of 0.905, L1-logistic inverse-regularization \(C_{\mathrm{SDCS}}=0.191\), coefficient-selection threshold \(|w_j|>10^{-5}\), \(\tau_{\mathrm{stab}}=0.705\), \(w_m=0.318\), \(w_s=0.767\), \(\rho_{\max}=0.956\), and \(K=1,000\). Mutual information is estimated from at most 20,000 source-training observations.

\paragraph{Roy comparator.} \blue{The empirical comparator implements the all-manual-feature Random Forest configuration in Table II of Roy et al., rather than their separate TF--IDF configuration. Its 17 published URL descriptors are classified by 100 trees with seed 42. Standard RF settings and a fixed shortener-domain list specify implementation details not provided in that study. Comparisons therefore concern this published manual-feature configuration under the common evaluation protocol.}

\section{Detailed Ablation Results}

\subsection{Complete SDCS procedure}

Given the training-only feature pool $F_{\mathrm{all}}$, labels $Y$, stability iterations $S$, inverse-regularization $C_{\mathrm{SDCS}}=0.191$, stability threshold $\tau_{\mathrm{stab}}$, weights $(w_m,w_s)$, redundancy threshold $\rho_{\max}$, and selection limit $K$, SDCS performs the following operations:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Draw $S$ randomized training subsamples, fit L1-regularized logistic regression to each, and compute the selection frequency $\operatorname{Stab}(f)$ for every feature using coefficients satisfying $|w_j|>10^{-5}$.
\item Retain $F_{\mathrm{stable}}=\{f:\operatorname{Stab}(f)\geq\tau_{\mathrm{stab}}\}$.
\item Compute normalized mutual information and \blue{LightGBM gain} on $F_{\mathrm{stable}}$, and combine them as $\operatorname{HIScore}(f)=w_m\operatorname{Imp}_{\mathrm{mi}}(f)+(1-w_m)\operatorname{Imp}_{\mathrm{gbt}}(f)$.
\item Rank features by $C(f)=w_s\operatorname{Stab}(f)+(1-w_s)\operatorname{HIScore}(f)$.
\item Traverse the ranking and retain a feature only when its absolute Pearson correlation with every previously retained feature is below $\rho_{\max}$; stop when $K$ features have been retained or the ranking is exhausted.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Temporal and Failure-Case Evaluation}

The prospective temporal cohort contains 290 confirmed OpenPhish URLs collected after the training cutoff. Because it contains no benign examples, only recall and score distributions are identifiable. LitePhish detects 256 URLs and misses 34, giving 88.28\% recall (Wilson 95\% CI: 84.06--91.49\%). The mean and median phishing probabilities are 0.868 and 0.996, respectively. The time-indexed LitePhish model trained on PhishFusion obtains 97.45\% recall on its internal positive test subset, which provides the in-distribution reference. {\color{bodycolor}Failure-stratum and counterfactual analyses use a separate seed-42 domain-disjoint model with 96.76\% phishing recall; each reference value is therefore interpreted within its own analysis.}

Counterfactual transformations probe sensitivity while preserving the original labels. A shortening-service proxy reduces recall from 0.9676 to 0.5535; clean-surface transformation reduces it to 0.7968; a reputable-host proxy reduces it to 0.9492; masking brand tokens in 1,035 applicable URLs reduces recall from 0.8850 to 0.8725. The popular-DNS stress cohort retains 89,937 names from the top 100,000 Umbrella ranks after exclusion of effective domains associated with known phishing samples. \blue{Each input is an HTTPS homepage URL constructed from a popular DNS name. These are popularity-based proxies, not individually verified benign browser visits.} At threshold 0.5, 74,175 names are labeled phishing, giving an 82.47\% proxy flag rate (Wilson 95\% CI: 82.22--82.72\%). \blue{This rate characterizes sensitivity to a homepage-dominated URL population; it is not a verified benign-navigation false-positive rate.}

\section{Resource and Energy Measurement}

The common latency protocol evaluates Ebbu, E2Phish, MUDS, TabNet, and LitePhish sequentially on identical fixed-seed URL cohorts, including each model's feature construction. A Linux cgroup enforces one CPU core and 512~MiB RAM on an Intel Xeon Gold 6240 host. Online measurements use batch size 1; batch measurements use 100 URLs. Warm-up observations are excluded. Peak resident memory includes the process and its descendants.

CPU-package energy is measured independently on an Intel Core i7-9700 desktop. Each model is pinned to one core and processes 5,000 unique URLs in batches of 100 for five randomized trials. Two warm-up passes are excluded. Each active phase lasts at least 33.6~s and contains at least 149 hardware-monitor samples; an adjacent 10~s idle phase provides the baseline subtraction. The resulting values exclude display, network, storage, power-supply losses, and other system components and therefore quantify CPU-package rather than whole-device energy.

\section{Paired Statistical Comparisons}

Prediction-level comparisons span the three internal domain-disjoint datasets, four cross-dataset transfers, and the external cohort. Accuracy differences use exact McNemar tests and paired multinomial-bootstrap intervals; phishing-class F1 uses paired multinomial bootstrap; ROC-AUC uses paired DeLong tests. Holm correction is applied within each table and metric family. The analysis covers 72 LitePhish--baseline scenario pairs, excluding the separately reported Roy comparator. On the external {\color{bodycolor}93,788-URL cohort (42,335 phishing and 51,453 benign)}, LitePhish exceeds all nine baselines in accuracy, phishing-class F1, and ROC-AUC after Holm correction. {\color{bodycolor}The direction and significance of differences vary across individual metrics and source--target settings. LitePhish obtains the highest phishing-class F1 in all four transfers, whereas DEPHIDES and URLNet obtain the highest ROC-AUC in the two Ebbu2017-source transfers; the interpretation therefore distinguishes F1 performance from ranking performance.}

\section{Interpretability Scope}

The keyword-frequency, suffix, and SHAP summaries characterize associations within the observed data. \section{Representation and Training Procedures}
\begin{bluechange}
The N-gram scorer combines normalized mutual information of observed count variables with absolute L1-logistic coefficients. The generic penalized loss is
\[
\min_{w,b}-N^{-1}\sum_i[y_i\log\sigma(w^Tx_i+b)+(1-y_i)\log(1-\sigma(w^Tx_i+b))]+\lambda\|w\|_1.
\]
The N-gram scorer uses liblinear $C_{\mathrm{ng}}=.5$; the SDCS recurrence models use $C_{\mathrm{SDCS}}=.191$. These inverse-regularization parameters specify their respective estimators; $\lambda$ denotes a generic penalty coefficient rather than a numerical conversion between estimator conventions. Each normalized score divides by the maximum over the candidate pool.
\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\footnotesize
\caption{N-gram Feature Selection}
\label{alg:ngram}
\DontPrintSemicolon
\SetKwInOut{KwData}{Input}
\SetKwInOut{KwResult}{Output}

\KwData{Training corpus $\mathcal{D}$ with labels $Y$, minimum document count $d_{\min}$ and maximum document-frequency fraction $r_{\max}$, variance threshold $\tau$, inverse-regularization $C_{\mathrm{ng}}$, weighting factor $\beta$, top-$K_{\text{ng}}$ limit}
\KwResult{Selected n-gram subset $F_{\text{ng}}^{\text{sel}}$}

\emph{\textbf{\# N-gram Extraction and Filtering}}\;
Extract character n-grams ($n \in \{2, 3\}$) from $\mathcal{D}$ to form initial set $F_{\text{ng}}$\;
Filter $F_{\text{ng}}$ by document frequency to obtain $F_{\text{ng}}^{\text{freq}}$\;
Apply variance thresholding on $F_{\text{ng}}^{\text{freq}}$ to obtain candidate set $F_{\text{ng}}^{\text{var}}$\;

\vspace{0.2cm}
\emph{\textbf{\# Feature Scoring}};
Compute Mutual Information $\mathbb{I}(f_j; Y)$ for each $f_j \in F_{\text{ng}}^{\text{var}}$;
Train one L1-regularized Logistic Regression model on $F_{\text{ng}}^{\text{var}}$ using $C_{\mathrm{ng}}=0.5$ to obtain coefficients $\{w_j\}$;
\For{each feature $f_j \in F_{\text{ng}}^{\text{var}}$}{
Normalize scores: $\text{MI}_{\text{norm}}(f_j)$ and $\text{Coef}_{\text{norm}}(f_j)$;
Compute composite score $\text{Score}_{\text{ng}}(f_j)$;
}

\vspace{0.2cm}
\emph{\textbf{\# Selection and Output}}\;
Select top $K_{\text{ng}}$ features based on $\text{Score}_{\text{ng}}(f_j)$ to form $F_{\text{ng}}^{\text{sel}}$\;
\KwRet $F_{\text{ng}}^{\text{sel}}$\;
\end{algorithm}
\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\footnotesize
\caption{Model Training}
\label{alg:focal_lgbm}
\DontPrintSemicolon
\SetKwInOut{KwData}{Input}
\SetKwInOut{KwResult}{Output}

\KwData{Scaled features $\mathbf{X}$, binary labels $\mathbf{y} \in \{0,1\}$, focusing parameter $\gamma \geq 0$, balancing parameter $\alpha \in (0,1)$, learning rate $\eta > 0$, boosting rounds $T$}
\KwResult{Trained focal-loss-inspired LightGBM ensemble model $\hat{f}$}

\vspace{0.2cm}
\SetKwFunction{FMain}{CustomLoss}
\SetKwProg{Fn}{Function}{:}{}
\Fn{\FMain{$\mathbf{y}$, $\mathbf{z}$}}{
    \emph{\textbf{\# Element-wise probability conversion and clipping}}\;
    $\epsilon \gets 10^{-15}$\;
    $\mathbf{p} \gets \operatorname{clip}\!\left(
    \frac{1}{1 + \exp(-\mathbf{z})},\, \epsilon,\, 1-\epsilon
    \right)$\;

    \emph{\textbf{\# Class-specific quantities}}\;
    $\mathbf{p}_t \gets \mathbf{y} \circ \mathbf{p} + (1 - \mathbf{y}) \circ (1 - \mathbf{p})$\;
    $\boldsymbol{\alpha}_t \gets \mathbf{y} \cdot \alpha + (1 - \mathbf{y}) \cdot (1 - \alpha)$\;

    \emph{\textbf{\# Focal weight}}\;
    $\mathbf{w} \gets (1 - \mathbf{p}_t)^{\gamma}$\;

    \emph{\textbf{\# Gradient and Hessian (diagonal approximation)}}\;
    $\mathbf{g} \gets -\boldsymbol{\alpha}_t \circ \mathbf{w} \circ (\mathbf{y} - \mathbf{p})$\;
    $\mathbf{h} \gets \max\!\left(
    \boldsymbol{\alpha}_t \circ \mathbf{w} \circ \mathbf{p} \circ (1-\mathbf{p}),
    \,10^{-6}
    \right)$\;

    \KwRet $\mathbf{g}, \mathbf{h}$\;
}

\vspace{0.2cm}
\emph{\textbf{\# Boosting initialization}}\;

$f^{(0)}(x)\gets0$\;

\For{$t = 1$ \KwTo $T$}{
    $\mathbf{z} \gets f^{(t-1)}(\mathbf{X})$\;
    $(\mathbf{g}, \mathbf{h}) \gets \texttt{CustomLoss}(\mathbf{y}, \mathbf{z})$\;
    Train weak learner $h_t(x)$ using gradients $\mathbf{g}$ and Hessians $\mathbf{h}$\;
    $f^{(t)}(x) \gets f^{(t-1)}(x) + \eta \cdot h_t(x)$\;
}

\KwRet Final model: $\hat{f}(x) = f^{(T)}(x)$\;
\end{algorithm}
\end{bluechange}
